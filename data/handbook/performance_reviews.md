# Performance Review Policy

## Review cycle

AsterPeak Solutions uses a yearly performance cycle supported by regular manager conversations. Goals are normally set in January, a mid-year check-in occurs in June or July, and the year-end review is completed in December. Employees who join late in the year may receive a shortened review or a development-only conversation.

Managers and employees should not wait for the formal cycle to discuss priorities, feedback, workload, or development. Goals may be revised when business needs change, but changes should be documented in the performance system so the employee understands what will be assessed.

## Assessment

Year-end reviews consider results, quality, collaboration, customer impact, demonstration of company values, and growth appropriate to the role. Evidence should cover the review period rather than only recent events. Managers should consider relevant context and avoid rating people based on protected characteristics, approved leave, accommodations, or personal similarity.

Employees complete a self-review and may identify achievements, challenges, feedback, and development goals. Managers provide a written assessment and discuss it with the employee. Calibration meetings may compare evidence and expectations across similar roles to improve consistency. Calibration does not require identical ratings for everyone.

The standard rating scale is: Exceptional Impact, Strong Impact, Successful, Developing, and Unsatisfactory. A rating describes performance for a period; it is not a permanent label.

## Pay and promotion

A performance rating is one input to compensation and promotion decisions, but it does not guarantee a salary increase, bonus, or promotion. Decisions also consider role scope, market position, internal equity, company performance, available budget, conduct, and local requirements. Managers must not promise an increase before formal approval.

Promotion requires sustained performance at the next level, an organizational need, and an approved role and budget. Long service alone does not guarantee promotion. Employees seeking growth should discuss expected skills and evidence with their manager and create a development plan.

## Concerns and support

An employee who believes a review contains a factual error should first ask the manager for clarification and provide relevant evidence. If the issue is unresolved, the employee may raise an HR ticket within 15 calendar days of receiving the review. HR can check whether the process was followed but does not automatically replace a manager’s judgment.

Performance improvement plans define specific concerns, expected standards, support, checkpoints, and a review period. They are not automatic termination decisions, but failure to improve may lead to further action subject to policy and law. The assistant cannot assess whether a rating is fair, predict an employment outcome, or coach a manager to manufacture evidence.

Harassment, discrimination, retaliation, or conflicts of interest should be reported under the Code of Conduct rather than treated only as a rating disagreement. Employees should not paste confidential review text, colleague feedback, or medical details into the chatbot.
